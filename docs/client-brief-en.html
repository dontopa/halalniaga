01 · Executive summary

One record that connects the stall to its sources.

HALALNiaga is a digital ecosystem for small food vendors: hawkers, stalls, food trucks, caterers and night-market traders. It brings together nearby certified or verified wet-ingredient suppliers, practical halal training, purchase records, self-assessment and programme recognition.

The prototype is built as a PWA: installable from the browser, usable offline after the first visit, and powered by local demo data. No backend, payment or real account is required.

Client brief

  • Wet ingredients first. Chicken, fish, vegetables, eggs and milk — the categories that are hardest to trace.
  • PWA, not an app store. The prototype can be installed and used on a phone.
  • Limited budget. Static hosting, local data and no server bill.

02 · The problem

The gap is not intention. It is evidence.

Small vendors want to do the right thing. What they lack is a practical way to prove what they already do — and a clear next step when something is missing.

01

Suppliers are hard to verify

Certificates sit in drawers or inboxes, rarely at the 6am wet-market decision about which chicken, fish or vegetable crate to buy.

02

Halal knowledge is uneven

Many still reduce halal to “no pork, no alcohol”, missing slaughter, storage and separation.

03

Certification feels out of reach

A small stall assumes the official process is too complex or expensive to even explore.

04

No purchase records

Without receipts or supplier references, nobody can prove where the chicken, fish or produce came from.

05

Cross-contamination is invisible

Shared boards, knives and oil are common — and almost never documented or corrected.

06

Wet ingredients move faster than paperwork

Chicken, fish, vegetables, eggs and milk are bought several times a week, often from different stalls. A record built for monthly invoices cannot keep up with a wet market.

Do not turn a small vendor into an administrator. Build the record from the work they already do — buy, cook, store, clean, serve.

The programme supports this chain through training, self-assessment, inspection and recognition. HALALNiaga records and monitors — it does not issue a halal certificate.

Design principle

Compliance is assessed on evidence, not identity. Malaysian halal certification is open to Muslim and non-Muslim operators who meet the requirements. This record is designed to support compliance, not replace the official process.

07 · Recognition model

Recognition that has to be maintained.

Tiers are not permanent labels. The evidence behind them expires — and the record shows when it needs renewal.

Verified + programme inspection passed within 180 days + re-assessment in the current cycle.

The important boundary

HALALNiaga does not issue halal certificates, does not use the Malaysian halal logo, and does not replace inspections by JAKIM or the state Islamic authority. Every QR record states this.

09 · Wet-ingredient focus

Wet ingredients come first.

Chicken, fish, vegetables, eggs and milk are bought several times a week and change suppliers. They are the hardest categories to trace — and the most important to monitor.

Why wet ingredients?

  • Short buying cycle: stock changes daily, not monthly.
  • Higher cold-chain and cross-contamination risk.
  • Supplier certificates can expire or change scope.
  • Customers are more likely to ask where the chicken, fish and vegetables came from.

In the app

The Wet ingredients button filters five wet-ingredient suppliers. The list is sorted nearest first, with distance and a “Nearby” marker for sources within 5 km.

11 · Limits & next steps

A prototype that is honest about its limits.

This prototype demonstrates the service model and user flow. It is not a production compliance system, and it does not make certification decisions.

Prototype limits

  • Data is stored in the device browser; it is not synced between devices.
  • No backend, real auth, payments or email.
  • Supplier certificate status is illustrative, not a live JAKIM lookup.
  • Scanner and mentor are simulated.
  • Programme inspections are recorded as a simulation.

Next steps

  • D1/Workers or Supabase backend for accounts and shared records.
  • Supplier onboarding and certificate upload flow.
  • JAKIM halal directory integration where authorised access exists.
  • Signed QR and an immutable audit trail.
  • Physical programme: training, inspection and field recognition.
Programme recognition, not a halal certificate.

Malaysian Halal Certification (SPHM) is issued by JAKIM or a recognised state Islamic authority. HALALNiaga supports the compliance journey — it does not replace the official process.
